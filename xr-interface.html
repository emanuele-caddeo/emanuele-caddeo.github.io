An Interface for Creating Procedures for Professional Training in XR

Research on enabling end-user development of professional XR training procedures directly from an Oculus headset, removing the need to switch between devices during authoring.

Featured image · video · GIF

Overview

This research was conducted as part of my Bachelor's thesis at the CG3 & HCI Laboratory of the University of Cagliari, in the context of Human–Computer Interaction and extended reality. The central problem it addresses is a common friction point in XR authoring workflows: when content creators need to design training procedures for AR/VR systems, they typically must switch between the headset and an external computer or tablet to configure steps, assets, and interactions.

The thesis proposes and implements an in-headset interface that allows users to create and edit professional training procedures entirely from within the Oculus environment. This reduces context switching, keeps creators in the spatial context of the content they're building, and lowers the barrier for non-technical professionals to author XR training experiences.

The work involved designing the interaction paradigm for 3D UI controls, evaluating usability with users from different professional backgrounds, and iterating on the interface based on findings. The research contributes to the broader field of end-user development (EUD) in immersive environments.
